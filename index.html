LAYER_01 ABOUT

Half engineer, half world-builder.

I spend most of my time in the space between engineering and design — writing the systems that let a player walk across a hillside and believe it was always there. Terrain that scatters itself. Cameras that breathe. Controls that disappear into the hand holding the phone.

LAYER_02 SELECTED WORK
01 — In development

VoidAnsh

A mobile exploration‑adventure built in Unity. Rolling procedural terrain, hand‑placed biomes, and a scenery system that scatters foliage and rock across the world so no two hillsides read the same. On‑screen joystick and swipe camera replace the keyboard entirely.

  • Unity · C#
  • Procedural scattering
  • Mobile touch controls

Realtime Shader Studies

An ongoing sandbox of GPU‑driven experiments — flow‑field particle systems, distortion shaders, and camera‑reactive geometry, most of which quietly end up inside whatever I'm building next.

  • Three.js · GLSL
  • GPU particles
  • Pointer‑reactive motion
03 — Internal tooling

Scenery Scatter Toolkit

An in‑editor tool that distributes vegetation and rock formations across generated terrain using density masks and jittered noise — built so level design stays a creative pass, not a placement chore.

  • Unity Editor tooling
  • Noise‑based placement
  • Density painting
LAYER_03 HOW I BUILD
Terrain first

The ground gets built before anything stands on it. Movement, camera, and pacing all take their cues from the shape of the land.

Systems over set‑pieces

I'd rather write a scattering algorithm than place a thousand trees by hand — then hand‑tune the result until it feels authored.

Touch is the interface

Every control is designed for a thumb first. If it needs a keyboard to feel good, it isn't finished.

Motion with intent

Animation should explain something — depth, weight, cause and effect — never just decorate a frame.
